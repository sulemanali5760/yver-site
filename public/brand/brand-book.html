YVER
Chapters of the World

Outerwear told in chapters. Each one begins with a real place and the craft people built there to live with cold.

For the team, the atelier and anyone photographing or filming for YVER. Section numbers are for reference: "see 6.3" means the Skrei shot list.

1Name, trademark & domain

YVER is the medieval French spelling of hiver, winter. It survives in Charles d'Orléans' 15th-century poem "Yver, vous n'estes qu'un villain" — "Winter, you are nothing but a villain". Four letters, one season, and a line under it: Chapters of the World.

Say it the French way, "ee-VAIR". Because English speakers may hesitate, spell it out in the first frame of any film and in the first line of a press note.

2Logo

A spaced serif wordmark in the tradition of tailoring houses, and a monogram: a chain-stitched Y inside a globe. The orange knot marks where the thread ends. The orange tick at the top points north.

2.2 Rules

Clear spaceKeep empty space around the logo equal to the height of the letter Y.
Minimum sizeWordmark 24 mm / 120 px wide. Monogram 8 mm / 24 px. Below that, drop "Chapters of the World".
ColoursCream on dark, ink on paper. Orange only for the knot and the north tick.
Don'tStretch it, add shadows or gradients, change the letter spacing, or set it in another typeface.

Files are in kalt-studio/brand/: wordmark and monogram, each on dark and light. The wordmark uses live fonts. Convert the text to outlines before print or embroidery, and have a designer refine the letter spacing by hand.

6Photo & film brief

We photograph two things: the pieces, exactly and consistently, and the places, honestly.

6.2 Product shots must line up with the site

  • Front view, camera at chest height, lens straight on. No angle.
  • Collar top at 7% of the frame height, shoulder seams at 12%, centre line exactly in the middle.
  • Frame ratio 10:11 (e.g. 3000 × 3300 px). Sleeves hang straight and reach the side edges.
  • Shoot on a neutral grey backdrop and deliver a cut-out PNG with a transparent background as well.
  • Colour: grey card in the first frame of every setup. Same light for every piece in a chapter.

6.3 Shot list · Chapter I, Skrei (Lofoten)

  1. Stockfish racks against the mountains. Wide shot at blue hour, racks full, no people.
  2. Rack detail. Rows of drying fish, frost if possible. Macro and medium.
  3. Harbour at first light. Boats coming in during the skrei season.
  4. Red rorbuer on stilts. Reine or Hamnøy, from the water side.
  5. Hands at work. Real fishery or processing workers, only with their consent and credit.
  6. Hjell Parka on a person walking past the racks. Full length, then medium.
  7. Northern Route Bomber at the harbour edge. The gold route line must read clearly.
  8. Rorbu Chore Coat against a red cabin wall. Colours must match, not clash.
  9. Archive Coat alone on the ghost form, on location, at dusk.
  10. Aurora, only if it happens. Never composite a fake sky.
  11. Film loops: sea swell, drying fish swaying, snow on a rack, a needle through wool (in the atelier).
  12. Sound: harbour wind, gulls, a boat engine at a distance.

6.4 Look and light

DoLow winter light, blue hour, real weather. Show fabric texture as it is. Leave space for text on the left or right of wide shots.
Don'tHeavy retouching of fabric, fake snow, composited skies, or staged "locals" as props.
Best timeFebruary to March: the skrei season, racks full, and a few hours of low daylight each day.
PeopleReal residents only with a signed model release, a fee and a credit. Say who they are; don't dress them up as a story.

6.5 Permissions and rights

  • Model releases for every recognisable person. Property releases for cabins and fish companies, since the racks are private property. Don't touch the fish: it's food.
  • Drones: check the rules of the Norwegian Civil Aviation Authority (Luftfartstilsynet) before flying.
  • Usage rights: website, social media and print for at least [x years], worldwide. Agree on a fee for paid advertising separately.
  • Credit the photographer on the chapter page.

7Voice

Write like a well-travelled friend who checked their facts: calm, specific, never grand.

DoName places and crafts precisely, and explain local words once ("hjell, the drying racks").
DoSay "inspired by" unless the piece was made there, by the people named.
DoCredit every maker and source by name.
Avoid"exotic", "tribal", "primitive", "ethnic", "timeless", and "authentic" without proof.

7.1 Example

Not this"Ancient Nordic fishermen's secrets, woven into a timeless masterpiece."
This"The grid of Lofoten's drying racks, chain-stitched across a boiled-wool parka. Inspired by the cod fishery; made by [atelier]."
